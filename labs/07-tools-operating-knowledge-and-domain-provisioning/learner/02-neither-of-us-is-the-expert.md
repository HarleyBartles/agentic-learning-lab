# 02 — Neither of us is the expert

Phase 1 started with domain expertise already in the room.

Now remove that advantage.

Choose a domain where you and the facilitator both have enough understanding to judge the work, but neither of you should be treated as the final authority.

The worked example is original prose or novel writing. If that is not a good fit, use another shared domain.

## Baseline

Ask the agent to perform one small bounded task.

If you are using the writing example, ask for an original short scene or passage.

Inspect the result together.

Do not ask only `is this good?`

Ask what you can actually point to:

- What feels padded or repetitive?
- What feels vague or overly abstract?
- Which parts are clear and direct?
- Which word choices feel strong or weak?
- Does the structure flow?
- Does anything sound impressive without saying very much?
- What do you disagree about?

Neither of you needs to pretend to be an expert.

## Import expertise

Now give the worker externally sourced expertise appropriate to the domain.

For the writing worked example, use the `writing-with-clarity` skill supplied by the facilitator.

The important fact is:

> **Neither of us wrote the expertise.**

The skill author did not need to become the original domain expert before making that knowledge useful to an agent. They sourced the expertise, operationalised it into reusable agent guidance, checked enough of the source to verify that the transformation was faithful enough to use, and provisioned it to the worker.

Ask the agent to revise the baseline or perform a closely comparable task with that expertise available.

## Compare

Look at the two results side by side.

What changed concretely?

For prose, you might notice:

- tighter sentences;
- fewer unnecessary modifiers;
- more direct claims or actions;
- more deliberate word choice;
- clearer paragraph flow;
- different emphasis or rhythm.

Also ask whether anything became worse.

A rule that improves clarity might still damage voice, humour, rhythm, atmosphere, or another purpose you care about.

## The point

Try saying this in your own words:

> **Neither of us needed to be an expert. Neither of us became an expert. We made the agent an expert anyway.**

Then answer one more question:

> Does the agent now get to decide whether its output is the result we wanted?

No.

Provisioning can improve the worker. The human still decides whether the result is fit for purpose.

Keep that distinction for the next phase.

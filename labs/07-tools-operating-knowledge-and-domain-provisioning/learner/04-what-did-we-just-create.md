# 04 — What did we just create?

Look back across the three phases.

## Phase 1

Software engineering had reliable expert judgment available from the facilitator.

Ask:

- What did expert supervision add even though the model already knew a lot?
- Which repeated corrections became worker guidance rather than repeated conversation?

## Phase 2

Neither person was the final domain authority.

Ask:

- Where did the extra expertise come from?
- Did either human need to become the expert first?
- What changed when external expertise became operational guidance for the agent?
- Who still decided whether the result was actually acceptable?

Keep the line:

> **Neither of us needed to be an expert. Neither of us became an expert. We made the agent an expert anyway.**

And the boundary:

> **The agent does not get to mark its own homework.**

## Phase 3

You knew the domain better than the facilitator.

Ask:

- What could you see that the facilitator might have accepted?
- What part of your expertise had to move into the worker's environment?
- Did the worker need knowledge, capability, workflow, verification, or some combination?

## Separate the layers

Across the lab you have touched several different things:

```text
model
what broad knowledge and reasoning it already brings

+ tools / capabilities
what it can actually do

+ instructions
what rules apply

+ skills / workflows
how recurring work should be approached

+ domain material
what competent work means here

+ project state
what is true about this job

+ quality criteria
what we are checking for

+ human verification
whether the result is actually fit for purpose
```

Try to explain these distinctions in your own words:

> **Knowledge is not capability.**

> **Capability is not domain provision.**

> **Apparent competence is not verification.**

> **Expertise should be provisioned, not repeatedly performed.**

## Final question

You have taken a model and deliberately equipped the environment around it with capabilities, operating knowledge, domain expertise, state, and ways to judge the work.

> **What exactly did we just create?**

Do not worry about finding the perfect technical definition yet.

Keep your answer. The next lab starts there.
